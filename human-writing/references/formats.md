# Different Forms Change Clothes

This file only handles form differences. User-specified forms override default long-post approach; speaker, materials, development and fact boundaries still follow main process. Poetry, scripts, dialogue and experimental writing aren't forced into modern prose's complete spine and paragraph shape.

## Zhihu Answers

First catch the question. If readers ask "why," get explanation out early. If they ask "what's it like," body needs experience, observation or clear note of where the knowledge comes from.

Can be very short or long. Don't thank for invite first, don't preview "I'll answer from three angles," don't rework answers into WeChat articles then paste back.

Professional questions—teach to usable point. Personal questions—clarify whose experience it applies to; don't write one experience as life rule.

## Forum Long Posts

Say why you're opening this post, then follow time, investigation or your lived process. Allow tangents, adds, backtrack corrections and pauses between stages.

When continuing series, each update must have new facts, new progress or new understanding. Don't use "if you're reading I'll continue" to fake interaction.

Real experience keeps what you didn't know then. Today's understanding can't trickle back as already-clear then.

## WeChat Articles and Blog Long Pieces

Body can be tighter than forum posts; still keep the speaker. Pick titles last; find them from already-written solid ground.

Long pieces lean on materials, explanation and process. Last thousand words keeping one opinion louder—delete or research more.

Small headers locate content; don't pre-write conclusions for every section. Different sections don't need equal length.

## Character and History Long Pieces

Verify with timelines; write by understanding order readers need.

A resume section only expands if it changes later conditions. Schools, positions and cities all written; character still hasn't moved—shows summary more than story.

Drama comes from documented change. Can't make every graduation, move and job switch into fate-bet.

## News, Products and Industry Analysis

First write what just happened; then explain why ordinary people need to care.

Release materials solve what the publisher wants to say. Authors add use, deployment, pricing, data, responsibility, swap-out cost and unanswered questions.

List companies until trend is proven—stop. Pick two or three different-role characters; explain impact clearly; rest in one sentence.

## Opinions and Commentary

Drop disputes into concrete facts, causality or value choices. Treat the strongest counter-argument accurately first; then give your current judgment.

No personal history: don't make up "I saw it myself." How you pick materials, explain causality, and take responsibility already forms voice.

## Personal Narratives

Find the clearest action, object, scene and one surprise. Keep your then-understanding; also show your today-looking-back shift.

When objects return, both real function and later meaning must hold. Don't forge a cup, ticket or old letter just for first-last echo.

## Short Pieces and Social Content

Only one main job each. Deliver news, show one observation, give one judgment, leave one detail, or run character through small change.

First sentence reaches object, action or result fast. Short pieces don't need forced reversals. Let last sentence land the meaning.

When cutting long pieces to short, re-gather and re-order materials; don't trim the original one path.

## Tutorials and Methods

Early say: what you'll have after, what you need, easiest failure point.

Steps follow dependency order. Each step: action and visible result. Theory to readers' point; enough to handle common change.

Tutorials save steps already—valuable. No need to address cognition, life and era at ending.

## Reviews and Business Content

Personal test materials follow real tasks. Why start using, what steps taken, where it failed, what resulted, suits who.

With only official materials, write materials reading or test plan; can't upgrade to personal experience.

Cooperation info, public facts, personal test and author judgment must distinguish. Well-done limits usually build more trust than pure praise.

## Voiceovers, Podcasts and Speeches

Write for ears. New terms say full first time; pronouns can find their thing; numbers stay only what listeners must remember.

Can't say in one breath—break. Opening sets where and what's now; middle follows what listeners ask next.

Read full voice and time. Smooth on paper, awkward in mouth—still must fix.

## Scripts and Dialogue

Only write what's visible or hearable. Inner need becomes action, choice, pause or speakable content.

Know both sides want before each scene starts. Dialogue serves immediate goal; doesn't take turns explaining setting and theme.

## Poetry and Emotional Writing

Let feeling move. See something at opening; touched by something in middle; how that thing changed by ending.

Choose few mutually-known images. Line breaks change breath, stress or meaning—don't just chop prose short.
